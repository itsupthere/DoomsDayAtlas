What the research says about survivability

Factors that matter across all 14 scenarios

Total weight each factor carries summed over every scenario preset. Low density, local food, water and distance from targets come up again and again.

Data. Natural hazards, population, farm output, social vulnerability and community resilience come from the FEMA National Risk Index (county level). Each hazard score blends how often it strikes with how much it destroys when it does, log-scaled to 0–10 nationally. Threat zones (nuclear targets, fallout, reactors, Yellowstone ash, Cascadia, New Madrid, geomagnetic storms, metro exodus) are distance models built for this tool from public sources; fallout uses a simple westerly-wind plume, and real fallout depends on the weather that day. Grid, water stress, growing season and the 2050 climate trend are statewide estimates. Connecticut and Valdez-Cordova, AK are approximated from FEMA's newer boundaries. Use this to frame decisions, then confirm locally before you move.

Europe, Asia-Pacific and World. Country scores come from the INFORM Risk Index Mid-2026 (EU Joint Research Centre) and World Bank indicators for farmland, grain output, water stress and power access. Threat zones are sampled on a 1-degree grid across each country, so large countries are scored on all their territory. Taiwan is estimated (INFORM does not cover it) and small territories borrow their governing country's scores; both show hatched on the map. INFORM only publishes countries, so Europe is scored country by country rather than by region.
